# Quest for Everyday Heroes

> An immersive educational VR role-playing game developed as part of MSc research exploring how immersive technologies can support daily life skills in children with Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD).

## Research Project

**Research Title:** *Exploring the Application of Immersive Technologies in Educational Games to Improve the Daily Life Skills for Children with Autism Spectrum Disorder*

**Game Title:** *Quest for Everyday Heroes*

**Research Area:** Virtual Reality • Immersive Technologies • Educational Games • Autism Spectrum Disorder • Serious Games • Human-Computer Interaction

## About the Project

*Quest for Everyday Heroes* is an immersive educational VR role-playing game designed to provide children with Autism Spectrum Disorder with a safe and structured virtual environment in which they can practise everyday activities and social interactions.

The project explores the application of immersive technologies in educational games, with a particular focus on supporting the development and practice of daily life skills. Players progress through scenarios inspired by real-world activities and complete structured tasks through exploration, interaction with virtual objects, and engagement with non-player characters (NPCs).

The game is being developed as the practical component of my MSc in Computing by Research at Asia Pacific University of Technology & Innovation (APU).

## Project Goals

- Create immersive VR scenarios based on familiar everyday activities.
- Provide structured opportunities to practise daily life skills in a controlled virtual environment.
- Explore VR interaction design for educational and research-oriented experiences.
- Incorporate task-based gameplay, environmental interaction, and NPC-guided scenarios.
- Investigate how immersive educational games can complement existing approaches to learning and skills practice.

## Gameplay Structure

The game is organised as a progression through three environments. Each level introduces a different set of everyday activities and practical tasks while maintaining a structured gameplay loop built around guidance, interaction, task completion, and progression.

### Level 1 — Home & Morning Routine

The first level introduces the player to a familiar home environment and a sequence of morning activities.

**Task progression:**

`Bedroom → Brush Teeth → Get Dressed → Breakfast → Main Gate → Catch the Bus`

The level is designed around completing a morning routine in sequence, moving between areas of the home and interacting with the environment before leaving for school.

### Level 2 — School

The second level moves the player into a school environment and introduces educational and activity-based tasks.

**Task progression:**

`Classroom → Math Quiz → Archery Activity → Carry Basketball → Store Basketball → School Bus Stop`

This level combines classroom activities with physical and object-based tasks, requiring the player to move through the school environment and complete a structured sequence before progressing.

### Level 3 — Farm

The third level places the player in a farm environment and focuses on practical, goal-oriented activities.

**Task progression:**

`Deliver Basket → Water Plants → Carry Crates → Load the Truck`

The farm level expands the experience into tasks involving object handling, environmental interaction, and completing practical responsibilities in sequence.

## Core Gameplay Approach

Across the three levels, the experience follows a consistent structure:

**NPC / Environmental Guidance → Player Action → Task Completion → Progression**

The design uses familiar environments, clear objectives, sequential tasks, and interactive objects to create a structured VR experience. Different scenarios can involve navigation, object manipulation, educational activities, and NPC-guided instructions.

## Design & Development Process

The project follows an iterative design-to-development workflow:

1. **Environment Concept Design** — planning the layout and important locations for each level.
2. **Player Journey Design** — defining how the player moves through the environment and encounters each activity.
3. **Gameplay Flow Design** — mapping the order of tasks and progression conditions.
4. **Unity Implementation** — building the environments, interactions, task systems, NPCs, and gameplay logic.
5. **VR Integration & Testing** — adapting interaction and navigation for the target VR experience.
6. **Research Evaluation** — evaluating the designed experience within the approved research methodology.

## Research & Evaluation

The project forms part of an academic research study investigating immersive technologies and educational games for children with ASD. The research includes requirements gathering, iterative design and development, and evaluation of the VR experience.

Evaluation focuses on interaction with the designed scenarios and tasks, including measures such as task completion, attempts, engagement with activities, and interaction choices where appropriate to the study design.

## Repository Purpose

This repository is primarily a **research and development portfolio case study**. It documents the design process, gameplay structure, development workflow, technical systems, and selected non-sensitive implementation material from *Quest for Everyday Heroes*.

The complete research application, participant data, confidential research materials, proprietary or restricted assets, and any content that cannot be publicly distributed are intentionally excluded.

## Ethics & Privacy

This research involves a sensitive participant group. Participant privacy, informed consent, research ethics, and responsible handling of research data are central to the project.

No personally identifiable participant information, confidential study data, recordings, or protected research materials will be published in this repository.
